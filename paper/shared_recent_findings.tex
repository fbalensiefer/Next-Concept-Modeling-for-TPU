\subsection{Empirical dependency models on frozen reference codecs}
The next experiment fitted four tabular priors to the original 512 training
code observations of each of the 120 reference codecs: independent fine codes,
adjacent pairs, a conditional dependence tree and a joint fine-level table.
No neural or codec update was performed. Every conditional row receives total
symmetric Dirichlet pseudo-count one. Tree topology uses empirical conditional
mutual information from training codes only and is rooted at a center. This
is related to Chow--Liu approximation~\citep{chow1968}; the shared topology and
smoothed tables are not asserted to retain its unrestricted optimality guarantee.

A sampled fidelity filter requires validation sequence accuracy and training
collision ceiling at least 98\%. It flags 16 codecs and accepts 104, including
one subsequently found below the population reconstruction threshold. The
filter is not a population certificate. All 480 prior fits and all-codec
results remain archived; population probabilities are used only for evaluation,
not fitting, topology, smoothing selection or the eligibility filter.
\RecentPriorTable
Joint tables improve mean decoded TV over independent tables on every source.
On Markov data, the conditional tree instead beats the joint table, consistent
with a beneficial sparsity/estimation tradeoff. At length eight the independent,
pair, tree and joint families have 17, 25, 31 and 511 free parameters including
the coarse distribution. Independent, pair and joint sampling take two rounds;
observed center-rooted trees take three to six. Enumeration/fit timings are not
end-to-end generation latency. Full joint tables grow exponentially and remain
finite-domain controls, not a proposed general language architecture.

\subsection{Exact innovation coordinates and the alignment-target diagnosis}
For the tested symmetric binary Markov law, define
\begin{equation}
 y_1=x_1,\qquad y_t=x_t\oplus x_{t-1}\quad(t>1).
\end{equation}
The variables are independent, with probabilities 0.5 for the initial bit and
0.15 for subsequent flip indicators. Prefix XOR reconstructs every input.
For the noisy-parity law (90\% uniform even parity plus 10\% uniform), let
\begin{equation}
 y_i=x_i\ (i<L),\qquad y_L=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{L}x_i.
\end{equation}
The first variables are independent fair bits and the last is an independent
Bernoulli(0.05) error bit. Both are bijections; exhaustive enumeration verifies
exact reconstruction and factorization to floating-point roundoff. All latent
bits can be drawn independently, followed by deterministic decoding. Prefix
XOR admits linear work and logarithmic parallel depth via associative
scan~\citep{blelloch1993}. These are classical known-structure constructions,
not newly learned transforms or universal independence theorems.

For any finite bijection \(T\), distributional KL is invariant:
\begin{equation}
 D_{\rm KL}\!\left(P_X\middle\|(T^{-1})_\#q\right)
 =D_{\rm KL}(P_{T(X)}\|q).
\end{equation}
This removes codec collision error and makes representation choice directly
relevant to factorization. Arbitrary finite distributions need not admit
independent fixed-length coordinates under a deterministic bijection, and an
inverse can itself be costly. These simple sources therefore motivate
information-preserving baselines without establishing a solution for language.

A separate replayable diagnostic examines the historical alignment target.
The teacher sees shifted causal inputs, whereas the codec sees the full text.
Its complete projected target is identical for sequences differing only in
the final byte, although that byte retains 0.4293 conditional bits. PCA/head
reconstruction changes teacher predictive probabilities by only
\(2.33\times10^{-6}\) mean KL nats; alignment substantially improves feature
matching but cannot make this target information-complete. This identifies a
conditioning conflict, not a controlled causal explanation of all failures.

\subsection{Causal RVQ prior and end-to-end generation telemetry}
The reconstruction-only gate selected the seed-0 512-byte codec at byte error
rate 0.16011568099171897 and exact-window accuracy 0.0. Its stage perplexities
were 9.906031520677761, 15.14685696072266 and 14.976882163869677; normalized
codebook utilization was 0.8339535551097937 and the screening score was
0.8787270864765133. The strict loader accepted its parameter tree. The selector
report records \code{generation_eligible=false} because it does not score a prior or
generation quality; this is not a rejection of the later Phase 2 pair.

Phase 2 keeps the selected Phase 1 encoder, decoder and codebooks frozen and
fits a categorical causal Transformer to valid RVQ indices serialized in
level-major order. The 512-byte codec has level lengths \((128,256,512)\),
so the prior context contains 896 code positions with vocabulary size 16. This
causal index model is distinct from the shared level-wise NCM predictor, which
predicts spatial positions in a codebook level in parallel. The 3-step pilot
used seed 0, batch size one, Adam rate 0.003 and prior-loss weight one. At step
3, prior cross-entropy was 2.9945807456970215, reconstruction loss was
0.5021113753318787, weighted commitment loss was 0.8349403738975525 and total
loss was 4.331632614135742. These are final-minibatch values from a short run,
not a convergence result or held-out evaluation.

The Rust \code{ncm-run generate-pilot --mode rvq-prior} command strictly loads the
Phase 1 codec and Phase 2 prior, encodes the supplied byte prompt to known code
positions, autoregressively samples the remaining indices and decodes them
through the codec. The recorded run used temperature 0.800000011920929,
top-$k=50$, top-$p=0.949999988079071$ and seed 42. It retained the 56-byte
prompt and returned 32 continuation bytes from 896 RVQ code decisions. Mean
code log probability was -2.5274094066449573, mean sampling entropy was
2.4040825506672263 nats, and measured prior-plus-decoder time was
0.6235438750009052 seconds (0.31300058303168043 prior; 0.3105432919692248
decoder). Since the prior vocabulary has only 16 entries, top-$k=50$ retained
all codes; nucleus filtering still applied. Log probabilities use the raw prior
distribution, whereas entropy is computed after filtering. This single
first-call timing includes prior JIT compilation and is not steady-state
latency. The generated sample and complete score vector are archived in
\path{reports/generation-pilot/generated_samples.json}; settings and timing are in
\path{reports/generation-pilot/generation_metrics.json}.

This one-prompt, three-training-step run verifies strict checkpoint ingestion,
deterministic sampling, codec decoding and finite telemetry. Its generated
continuation is not fluent; no held-out generation quality, multi-prompt or
multi-seed benchmark, uncertainty estimate, matched baseline, or TPU result is
claimed. The selector's \code{generation_eligible=false} field remains scoped to its
reconstruction-only protocol; it does not evaluate the separate Phase 2 prior.

\subsection{Evidence scope, reproducibility and next steps}
The 480 fits reuse 120 codecs; objective arms share seeds and datasets. They
are not 480 independent replications. Paired analysis should treat student
seeds as replication units. The broader studies adapt separately to each
small binary source and retain the historical alignment convention and shared
regularizers. No pretrained transfer, language quality or TPU result follows.
Reconstruction constraints and uncertainty-aware acceptance should precede
quantizer replacement; sparse dependencies and small joint blocks are the
next tractable modeling controls.

For a deterministic encoder $z=E(x)$, the RVQ bound is
\[
-\log_2 p(x)\;\leq\;-\log_2 p(z)-\log_2 p(x\mid z).
\]
Dividing by 512 gives the reported bits-per-byte upper bound. The decoder
cross-entropy charges reconstruction error alongside prior error. Raw and XOR
controls report their fitted autoregressive model NLL, with lossless inverses.
The codec reaches 83.90\% byte accuracy but zero exact reconstructions among
66 held-out windows. This is a substantial fidelity bottleneck; the lower
surface bigram JSD of RVQ (0.253--0.308) does not establish better sequence
likelihood. These measurements do not isolate reconstruction as the sole
cause, and comparing upper bounds does not prove ordering of exact marginal
likelihoods. The Chow--Liu held-out gain over independent marginals is
$-2.3187$ bits/byte; it omits earlier-level conditioning and does not estimate
conditional total correlation or the conditioning-summary gap.

There is one distinct Gutenberg book per train, validation and test split.
The 66 test windows are non-overlapping and independent of the training book;
window standard errors are descriptive within one book, not evidence of
between-document generalization. Equal updates, batch size and transformer
dimensions do not equalize parameter counts or FLOPs. The recorded stop rule is
\code{halt\_quantizer\_expansion\_and\_pivot}: no RVQ candidate strictly improves
all three recorded coordinates over both lossless controls. This is a local
stop decision, not a proof that lossless controls dominate every RVQ point;
RVQ improves bigram fit, and Chow--Liu has lower recorded latency. Further
scaling requires a compute-normalized comparison across more documents and
validation of timing and dependency accounting.
