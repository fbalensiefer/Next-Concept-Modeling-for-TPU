\subsection{Evidence snapshot and completed engineering milestones}
All results in this section describe the implementation at revision
\code{1d600d7}, dated November 12, 2026, package version 0.1.0~\citep{localrepo}.
The recorded stack was Python 3.12.16, JAX/jaxlib 0.10.2, Flax 0.12.8,
Optax 0.2.8 and NumPy 2.5.4, on macOS 26.6.2 arm64 CPU, with float32 arrays.
The CPU model was not recorded; no TPU hardware was available.
The validation record reports \TestCases{} passing pytest cases, including
parameterized runs, exact checkpoint continuation and two virtual four-device
CPU sharding checks. Test runtime is not an algorithmic benchmark.

The completed engineering path includes UTF-8 byte loading and chunking,
configuration/data hashes, atomic checkpoint replacement, step-addressed random
batches, a frozen-codec prior, seeded categorical code sampling with temperature
and optional top-$k$, and byte decoding. Output length remains a caller input;
there is no prompt-conditioning, learned-length or EOS interface. The separate
26-byte training and 13-byte validation fixture reached 100\% training-byte
reconstruction by step 100 of a 500-step run. Validation CE increased from
1.949 nats at step 100 to 3.550 at step 500: this was overfitting, not a
successful held-out language result. The historical codec/prior logs are now
archived in \path{reports/text-experiments/}; their checkpoint hashes match the
CPU benchmark's source identities. Those records are distinct from the
finite-domain teacher studies below.

\subsection{CPU inference measurements}
\CPUBenchmarkTable
The benchmark timed lowered/compiled functions separately from warmed inference,
kept timed inputs on-device and waited for every output with
\code{jax.block_until_ready}. Sampling used temperature 1.0; host text conversion
and checkpoint I/O were excluded. NCM byte decoding and all cached AR serial
steps were included. With batch 16 and untrained length-512 models, actual-level
execution reduced median latency from \PaddedLatency{} to \ActualLatency{} ms.
The trained length-8 fixture stored 21,328 codec parameters, 4,032 prior
parameters and 768 codebook embedding elements, whereas AR had 11,904 parameters.
Neither parameter count nor quality was matched. Longer shape checks used
\(D=32,V=64,H=64,J=2\), four attention heads and levels
\((L/4,L/2,L)\); they were not trained on text.

Compiler live-buffer estimates, process-lifetime counters where supported,
compilation times and 18 separate profiling traces were recorded. Per-case
measured peak device memory was unavailable; compiler estimates are not peak
memory measurements. These observations establish a local implementation
improvement, not TPU acceleration or a quality-matched model speedup.

\subsection{One-teacher finite-domain protocol}
\ProtocolSeedsTable
Each domain input is a length-4 sequence over A/B (16 possibilities).
A locally trained causal byte Transformer has width 16, two heads and one block;
it is not an external pretrained language model. Its source distribution mixed
probability \(1-\rho\) equally over AAAA/BBBB with \(\rho=0.25\) uniformly over
all 16 sequences. After 400 teacher updates, its A/B logits were normalized at
every prefix to define an exactly enumerable law \(p_s\).
Teacher seed 2026 and its checkpoint remained fixed in every protocol.

Training, validation and calibration comprised 512, 512 and 256 independently
sampled sequence draws, respectively. They shared the finite support and could
repeat identical sequences across splits; there was no held-out domain or
larger-teacher evaluation. A sign-fixed PCA interface fitted on calibration
features mapped normalized causal teacher features to \(r=D=8\).
Student codebooks had \(K=2\), \((n_1,n_2)=(1,4)\), \(V=2\), and nominal
capacity 5 bits; student priors used width 16, two heads and one block.
The byte vocabulary was \(W=256\), with no fallback side channel in these studies.
The AR teacher used BOS ID 256 on its input only.

Arms were R (reconstruction/commitment/utilization), A (R plus representation
alignment), D (R plus dependence) and AD (both). Paired initial states,
minibatch order, architecture and update counts were fixed within each seed.
Every student used 400 codec updates and 400 frozen-codec prior updates, global
batch 64 and Adam learning rate 0.003. The three four-arm follow-ups additionally
used 200 decoder-only replay updates at the same rate. Prior parameters and code
assignments stayed fixed during replay. Shared teacher/calibration preparation
was charged equally; zero auxiliary coefficients still executed the common
student objective graph. Equality of compiler-estimated student step FLOPs is
not equality of measured total hardware work or a compute-saving claim.

\subsection{Completed controls, negative results and coverage repair}
\StudyResultsTable
The initial three-seed pilot passed its fixed AD-versus-A TV/dependence/fidelity
gates. Mean hard conditional TC fell from \PilotTCAlignment{} to
\PilotTCCombined{} nats. That comparison lacked a D-only control; most TV gain
came from one seed, and seed 0's AD model missed 5.12\% of teacher probability.
It therefore did not establish a reliable alignment contribution or full SCALE.

The four-arm decoder-replay protocol reserved student seeds 3--7. Its coverage
and reliable-alignment gates both failed. In AD seed 4, 16 inputs occupied only
10 codewords. The deterministic code-to-sequence reconstruction ceiling
\(\sum_i\max_{x:Q_\psi(x)=i}p_s(x)\) was 89.80\%; replay achieved 87.87\%
sequence reconstruction and still missed 6.63\% of teacher mass. More decoder
updates could not recover distinctions already merged into the same code.

The balanced-codec protocol reserved seeds 8--12. Codec reconstruction and
alignment sampled uniformly over distinct observed training sequences;
self-normalized training-frequency importance weights kept soft dependence
aimed at empirical teacher mass. Prior training retained the original empirical
frequency distribution. No population probabilities or validation examples
selected repair targets. All five new AD runs retained 16 encodings and achieved
100\% sequence reconstruction and complete output support, already before extra
replay. The known failing seed 4 also recovered under the changed sampler.
The reliable-alignment gate nevertheless failed: AD beat D on only 3/5 new seeds,
and A had slightly lower mean TV than AD. Thus the earlier dependence benefit
was not uniform across samplers and initializations.

\subsection{Coarse-codebook stabilization}
The maintained protocol reserved seeds 13--17; seeds 4, 8 and 10 were known-case
checks. It combined two initial encoder batches, 20 K-means++/Lloyd iterations,
independent hard-usage EMA decay 0.9, threshold 1 assignment per global batch,
25-step dead-code grace, reset count 2 and replacement noise 0.01.
It added coarse entropy coefficient 0.05, temperature 2.0 to 0.25 and commitment
0 to 0.25 over 200 steps, and centroid step scale 0.25. Fine-book EMA and neural
Adam rates were unchanged. All four arms executed the same maintenance logic.
The individual components were not separately ablated.

The fixed stability gate required both codes active, population coarse
perplexity at least 1.5, no more than 5\% collapsed minibatches after the warmups,
complete teacher output support and sequence reconstruction at least 98\%.
All \StabilityModels{} new-seed models (five seeds times four arms) passed.
Minimum population perplexity was \StabilityPP{} out of 2; each model recorded
zero collapsed steps over 200 post-annealing observations. Both codes remained
active and output support was preserved. AD minimum sequence reconstruction was
99.07\%; the other three arms reached 100\% on the new seeds.
All 32 runs, including known-case checks, had matching compiler-estimated
per-step FLOPs: 10,564,359 for codec training, 6,996,102 for prior training and
5,837,030 for decoder replay. Initialization used 128 valid pooled samples per
model and was shared per seed, with its budget charged equally.

The reliable teacher-alignment gate was distinct: AD had to beat D by mean TV
at least 0.02 and win on at least 4/5 new seeds, while preserving coverage.
It won \FinalAlignmentWins{}/5 but its mean improvement was only
\FinalAlignmentGain{}, so that gate still failed. Stable codebooks are an
engineering achievement, not evidence of cross-size transfer, reduced total
adaptation compute or general language-model quality. All gates were saved
locally before their study; no external preregistration is claimed.
